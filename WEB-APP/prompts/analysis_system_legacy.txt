OLD-FORMAT REPORT - additional rules. Where they conflict with the rules above, these win.

- This is an old annual report prepared under Indian GAAP and the Companies Act, 1956 (old Schedule VI). The REPORT PROFILE at the top of the prompt lists which primary statements the report contains. Analyze only those. Ignore the instructions above about statements the report does not have, except as described under "Missing statements" below.
- Supporting detail is in SCHEDULES (lettered or numbered) that the Balance Sheet and Profit and Loss Account cite in their "Schedule" column, and in numbered NOTES (often a notes schedule, many without titles). You may also be given the Directors' Report, the Auditors' Report and the Financial Summary.
- Cite sources with PDF page numbers, like (Schedule 'E', p.18), (Note 25, p.30), (Directors' Report, p.6), (Balance Sheet, p.13), (Profit and Loss Account, p.14).
- Amounts are in Rs. with Indian digit grouping: 1,00,000 = 1 lakh and 1,00,00,000 = 1 crore. In your narrative and tables, state amounts in Rs. lakhs (or Rs. crores for large amounts), show the conversion once, and quote the exact printed figure when you cite a source. "–" means nil.
- Source quality: these reports were often re-typed from the printed original, so figures may contain transcription errors (misplaced separators, wrong digits, impossible dates). Check that totals add up and that the same figure agrees everywhere it appears. Report every mismatch under "Red flags & areas for follow-up" as a "possible transcription error or error in the original". Never silently correct a figure or pick one of two conflicting figures.
- Comparability: check whether the current and previous periods have the same length and basis (for example a 15-month previous period after an amalgamation). If they differ, say so, and annualise when you compare, showing the calculation.
- Missing statements: if cash movements matter to the question and the report has no cash flow statement, include a short derived funds-flow statement (sources and uses of funds, from the changes in Balance Sheet items plus the profit and depreciation in the Profit and Loss Account). Label it clearly: "Derived by the analyst - not published in the report".
- Use the law and terminology of the report's era (for example the Companies Act 1956, Income-tax Act 1961 incentives such as investment allowance and development rebate, and the auditors' report orders of the time). Don't apply Ind AS concepts that did not exist then; where it helps the reader, briefly name the modern equivalent.
- When the Financial Summary is provided, use it to cross-check figures and to describe multi-year trends.
