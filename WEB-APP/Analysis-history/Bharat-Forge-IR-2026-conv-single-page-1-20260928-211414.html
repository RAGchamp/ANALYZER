Question

Analyze company's Commercial Vehicles business

Summary

  • The two CV businesses moved in opposite directions. Management reports Domestic CV revenue of ₹1,036 crore, up 8% YoY (MD&A, p.78). CV exports fell 34% to ₹1,324 crore (MD&A, p.76).
  • Combined CV revenue fell by about 20%, from roughly ₹29,684 million to ₹23,600 million (calculated below). The fall was driven by a 50% drop in North American CV revenue, which management attributes to de-stocking in the North American truck market (MD&A, p.76).
  • CV is a smaller part of the group. It is about 14% of consolidated revenue, down from about 20%.
  • The audited statements do not show CV separately. It sits inside the "Forgings" segment (Note 49, p.472). None of the CV figures can be tied directly to the audited numbers.

What management says

  • Domestic CV:
  • Management says GST rate cuts lowered the total cost of ownership, and fleet operators spent the savings on new trucks. Better roads and manufacturing growth also helped (MD&A, p.78).
  • The mix is moving to heavier trucks (tractor-trailers and tippers). Management says this favours suppliers like Bharat Forge as OEMs build "bigger and more sophisticated platforms" (MD&A, p.78; Board's report, p.88).
  • The Board's report adds that bus orders from state transport undertakings were strong and that CV OEMs had record EBITDA margins (p.88).
  • Export CV:
  • The business focuses on Class 7 and 8 trucks in the US and heavy trucks (HCV) in Europe.
  • It supplies safety-critical engine, driveline and transmission components.
  • Management describes itself as a "critical supplier" whose strategy is to gain market share. It states that US tariffs caused "no business disruptions" and "no impact on market share" (MD&A, p.76). This is management's claim; the report gives no market-share data to support it.
  • K Drive (acquired July 2025): it is building an axles portfolio "beyond M&HCVs". Management expects results in 2–3 years (Board's report, p.88).

Outlook and risks

  • Expectations:
  • ICRA, cited by management, expects the Indian auto industry to grow 3–6% in FY2027 with "stable demand in commercial vehicles".
  • The ₹12.2 lakh crore infrastructure outlay is expected to support demand (MD&A, p.77).
  • Risks management names:
  • A sharp rise in diesel prices "might adversely impact the CV growth outlook" (MD&A, p.77).
  • West Asia tensions, shipping costs and energy prices (MD&A, p.76).
  • Risks the text implies:
  • No timeline is given for North American de-stocking to end.
  • The domestic boost from the GST cut may not repeat.
  • Exports depend on a few large OEMs in cyclical Class 8 markets.

What the audited figures show:

  • Forgings segment: revenue grew (139,285.82 − 129,612.33) ÷ 129,612.33 = +7.5%. Its result rose to ₹19,560.91 million from ₹18,561.18 million, but the margin slipped from 14.3% to 14.0% (Note 49, p.473). A 20% fall in CV was therefore absorbed by other forgings businesses.
  • US revenue: it grew 3.4% even though management reports a 50% fall in North American CV. Non-CV US businesses must have offset the drop, but the report does not say which.
  • India revenue: it rose 43% (₹36,091.80 million to ₹51,692.92 million; Note 49, p.475). This probably includes the K Drive acquisition, but that is not confirmed in the notes provided.

Questions for management

  1. What is the CV EBIT margin, and how much did operating deleverage from the export fall cost the Forgings segment?
  2. What is the prior-year base for Export CV, and when do you expect North American de-stocking to end?
  3. Which US businesses offset the 50% CV decline, given that US revenue grew 3.4%?
  4. What evidence supports "no impact on market share" under US tariffs?
  5. How much of K Drive's ₹9,578 million revenue is M&HCV axles, and is it included in the Domestic CV figure?
