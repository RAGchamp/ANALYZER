Question

what percentage of shares was owned by Buffett partnership

Summary

  • As of April 7, 1969, Buffett Partnership, Ltd., a Nebraska limited partnership, owned about 69.99% of Berkshire Hathaway's outstanding shares, not counting treasury stock (Item 3(a), p.2).
  • Warren E. Buffett, a director of Berkshire, was the sole general partner of the partnership (Item 3(a), p.2). Through the partnership he controlled Berkshire.
  • The balance sheet shows 1,017,547 shares issued less 32,065 held in treasury, which leaves 985,482 shares outstanding (Balance Sheet, p.7). 69.99% of that is about 689,700 shares held by the partnership. This is my calculation; the report does not give the number of shares.
  • The partnership's holding does not appear in the audited statements. The only source is the Form 10-K text, which is not audited.

What management says

  • Item 3, "Parents and Subsidiaries of Registrant," names Buffett Partnership, Ltd. as the holder of about 69.99% of Berkshire's outstanding shares as of April 7, 1969. This makes the partnership Berkshire's controlling shareholder (p.2).
  • Berkshire in turn owns National Fire & Marine Insurance Company and 99% of National Indemnity Company. Neither was consolidated in 1968 (Item 3(b), p.2–3). The page 3 transcription says "National Fire & Indemnity Company" in one place, which looks like a transcription or typing slip.
  • Berkshire had 1,557 holders of record on April 4, 1969 (Item 1, p.2). There were no changes in outstanding equity securities during the year (Item 2, p.2).

Performance and drivers

Here is how I worked out the partnership's shares:

  • Shares outstanding = 1,017,547 issued − 32,065 treasury = 985,482 (Balance Sheet, p.7).
  • Partnership shares ≈ 985,482 × 69.99% ≈ 689,740, which rounds to about 689,700.
  • Everyone else ≈ 985,482 − 689,740 ≈ 295,700 shares (30.01%), spread across the other ~1,556 record holders.
  • Cross-check against earnings per share: net earnings of $4,662,336 ÷ 985,482 shares = $4.73, which matches the reported $4.73 (P&L, p.8). So the balance sheet share count is the right base for the percentage.
  • By the same arithmetic, the partnership's share of 1968 net earnings was about 69.99% × $4,662,336 ≈ $3.26 million.

The sections do not say when or how the partnership built up this stake.

Outlook and risks

  • The sections give no outlook about the partnership's holding.
  • Implied risk: one holder with about 70% controls board elections and strategy, so minority holders have little say.
  • The holding is dated April 7, 1969, after the December 28, 1968 year end. The year-end percentage is not stated, though with no change in outstanding shares (Item 2) it was probably similar.
