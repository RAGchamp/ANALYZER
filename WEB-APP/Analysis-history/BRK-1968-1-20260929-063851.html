Question

what percentage of shares was owned by Buffett partnership

Summary

  • The 10-K states that as of April 7, 1969, Buffett Partnership, Ltd. owned approximately 69.99% of Berkshire Hathaway's outstanding shares, excluding treasury stock (Item 3(a), p.2).
  • Buffett Partnership was a Nebraska limited partnership. Warren E. Buffett, a director of Berkshire, was its sole general partner (Item 3(a), p.2). That made him the controlling party.
  • Applying 69.99% to the 985,482 shares outstanding at year-end gives about 689,700 shares (calculated below). The 10-K does not state the share count itself.
  • Berkshire reported no change in its outstanding equity securities during the year (Item 2, p.2). So the year-end share base is a fair base for the April 1969 percentage.

What management says

  • The disclosure is under Item 3, "Parents and Subsidiaries of Registrant." The heading itself treats Buffett Partnership as Berkshire's parent (p.2).
  • Berkshire in turn owned all of National Fire & Marine Insurance Company and 99% of National Indemnity Company, both Nebraska insurers. Neither is consolidated in the 1968 statements (Item 3(b), p.2–3). Note that p.3 names the first company "National Fire & Indemnity Company", which looks like a slip in the original or the transcription.
  • The stock was widely held outside the partnership: there were 1,557 record holders of the $5 par common stock on April 4, 1969 (Item 1, p.2).

Performance and drivers

  • Outstanding shares = 1,017,547 issued − 32,065 in treasury = 985,482 (Balance Sheet, p.7).
  • Shares held by Buffett Partnership ≈ 985,482 × 69.99% ≈ 689,700. This assumes no change between December 28, 1968 and April 7, 1969, which Item 2 supports.
  • Check on the share base: net earnings of $4,662,336 ÷ 985,482 = $4.73 per share, which matches the reported $4.73 (P&L, p.8). So 985,482 is the correct denominator.
  • The partnership's share of book equity ≈ 69.99% × $36,205,271 ≈ $25.34 million (Balance Sheet, p.7).
  • Everyone else held about 30.01%, roughly 295,800 shares.

Outlook and risks

  • The filing says nothing about the partnership's plans for the stake.
  • The text implies a control concentration: at nearly 70%, one partnership run by one general partner, who also sits on the board, controls the company. Minority holders have little say.
  • The figure is a point-in-time holding (April 7, 1969), so it is not an audited year-end balance.
