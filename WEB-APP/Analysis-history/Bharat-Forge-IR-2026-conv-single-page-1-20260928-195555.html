Detailed analysis

1. Where the CV business sits

  • Note 1 (p.348) says the Group makes forged and machined components, including aluminium castings, "for the auto and industrial sectors", plus electric-vehicle components and defence products.
  • Note 49 (p.472) puts all automotive and industrial forgings in Forgings. CV is therefore inside Forgings, together with passenger vehicles and industrial customers.
  • The electric-vehicle and other new initiatives are probably in Others, but the note does not say so explicitly.
  • No product-line revenue is disclosed. Note 24 only splits revenue by type (goods, tooling, job work, scrap, export incentives), not by end market.

2. Forgings performance (the proxy)

  • Growth: external revenue grew 7.46%, compared with 11.17% for the Group. Forgings' share of gross segment revenue:
  • FY26: 139,285.82 ÷ 178,555.66 = 78.0%
  • FY25: 129,612.33 ÷ 156,852.15 = 82.6%
  • Margin: 14.04% against 14.32%. Profit grew more slowly than revenue (+5.4% vs +7.5%).
  • At Group level, raw materials rose from 42.6% of revenue (64,396.23 ÷ 151,228.03) to 46.3% (77,779.31 ÷ 168,116.53) (P&L p.342).
  • That fits with margin pressure, but it is not broken down by segment.
  • Capital employed and return (segment assets less segment liabilities):
  • FY26: 147,642.44 − 34,064.62 = 113,577.82, so return = 19,560.91 ÷ 113,577.82 = 17.2%
  • FY25: 132,272.20 − 28,754.18 = 103,518.02, so return = 18,561.18 ÷ 103,518.02 = 17.9%
  • Investment: additions of ₹18,879.40m are 2.3 times Forgings depreciation of ₹8,285.96m. The segment is investing for growth, but returns have not yet caught up.
  • Exceptional loss: ₹1,073.54m in Forgings. Note 32 was not provided, so its nature is unknown.
