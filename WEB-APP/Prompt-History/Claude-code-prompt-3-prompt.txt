  - It cannot be fully traced without invoice-level data, and FX translation may also affect it.
- Credit terms are "generally 30 to 240 days" (p.389). This wide range means a large "<6 months overdue" balance could represent debtors more than 14 months past invoice date.

Coverage = allowance ÷ gross in the same bucket (Note 12, p.389).

- **ECL on "considered good" balances** (total allowance less credit-impaired):
  - FY26: 567.08 − 164.46 = 402.62, which is **1.01%** of 39,669.29.
  - FY25: 592.32 − 154.48 = 437.84, which is **1.48%** of 29,569.38.
  - The provision fell 8.0% while the exposure rose 34.2%.
- **Coverage of balances overdue > 1 year** fell from 75.4% to 29.0%:
  - FY26: (110.70 + 62.43 + 181.12) = 354.25 ÷ 1,220.92 = 29.0%.
  - FY25: (66.35 + 70.52 + 160.88) = 297.75 ÷ 394.68 = 75.4%.
- **Allowance as a share of all overdue balances** fell from 5.50% (592.32 / 10,760.23) to 2.88% (567.08 / 19,722.71).
- **Coverage in the 1–2 year bucket is inconsistent.** The FY25 ECL rate on the 6–12 month "good" balance was 15.5% ((197.96 − 24.55) / 1,116.99). After those balances aged further, the ECL rate on 1–2 year "good" balances is only 12.4% ((110.70 − 7.07) / 835.27). Older debt normally attracts a *higher* provision rate, not a lower one.
- **Credit-impaired balances are stable** (+6.5% to ₹164.46 Mn) and are 100% provided.
  - FY25's 2–3 year impaired (35.75) plus >3 year impaired (65.58) = 101.33, against FY26 >3 year impaired of 95.91.
  - This suggests small write-offs or recoveries, but no movement schedule for the allowance is provided.

### 4. Bill discounting (transfers not derecognised)
- The Holding Company sold receivables **with recourse**. Because it keeps substantially all of the credit risk, the receivables stay on the balance sheet and the cash received is shown as a borrowing (Note 12, p.390; borrowings in Note 18, not provided).
- Transferred receivables are 15.8% of gross (6,311.82 / 39,833.75), against 15.4% in FY25 (4,572.27 / 29,723.86).
- Excluding transferred balances, net receivables still grew 34.2%:
  - FY26: 39,266.67 − 6,311.82 = 32,954.85.
  - FY25: 29,131.54 − 4,572.27 = 24,559.27.
  - So factoring is not masking the growth, but it does increase reliance on working-capital funding.

### 5. Other composition points
- **Secured receivables** grew 5.3x to ₹131.30 Mn. The amount is small (0.3% of gross), but the nature of the security is not disclosed.
- **Related-party receivables** are included in "considered good" but not quantified. The note refers to Note 48, which is not in the extract.
- **No disputed receivables** and no "significant increase in credit risk" balances are reported in either year (p.389).
- **Unbilled revenue** is reported as nil or blank in the ageing schedule.

### 6. What cannot be assessed from this extract

  - A split by geography or customer.
  - The ECL methodology.

## Red flags & areas for follow-up
1. **Sharp rise in overdue balances.** Overdue grew 83% (+₹8.96 Bn), and <6 month overdue nearly doubled, while not-yet-due grew only 6%. Ask management:
   - Is this timing (e.g., March-end billing, delayed customer payments in specific segments or geographies such as defence or export programmes)?
   - Or is it a deterioration in collections?
   - What has been collected after the reporting date?
2. **Falling provisions against an ageing book.** Total allowance fell 4.3% while gross receivables rose 34%, and balances >1 year tripled. Coverage fell sharply in every bucket older than 6 months (e.g., 1–2 years: 43.2% → 13.1%). Request:
   - The ECL provision matrix and any changes in assumptions.
   - Whether specific customers were reassessed or balances written off.
   - Whether FY25's 17.3% coverage on 6–12 month balances was released.
3. **Ageing from FY25's 6–12 month bucket.** About ₹835 Mn of "considered good" balances now sit at 1–2 years overdue, with only about 12% ECL. Identify the counterparties and whether they are related parties.
4. **Credit-impaired balance within "not yet due" (₹8.04 Mn).** Impairment before the due date implies specific customer distress. Ask which counterparty this is.
5. **Borrowing exceeds transferred receivables.** Bank borrowing exceeds the transferred receivables by ₹1.78 Mn in FY26 and ₹9.40 Mn in FY25 (p.390). Ask whether this is accrued interest or a timing difference.
6. **Ambiguous bill discounting disclosure.** The note describes the liability as a "secured/unsecured bank loan." Ask for the split, and cross-check against Note 18.
7. **Presentation issues in the source:**
   - On p.390 the lead-in sentence ("The following information shows…") appears after the table it introduces.
   - The FY25 "Unbilled revenue" row shows a single "-" while FY26 is blank.
   - These are cosmetic but suggest limited review of this disclosure.
8. **Secured receivables up 431%.** Ask what the security is (e.g., LCs or bank guarantees).
9. **Related-party receivables are not quantified in this note.** Review Note 48 for concentration and ageing of related-party balances.
10. **Obtain revenue** (from the Statement of Profit and Loss or segment note) to calculate DSO and test whether receivables growth is in line with sales.
