## Summary
- **The balance-sheet deferred tax liability fell 82%**, from ₹1,198.28m to ₹215.24m. The main cause is the tax effect of cash flow hedge losses booked in OCI (a ₹1,338.69m credit). The Group's net deferred tax position moved from a **net asset of ₹703.15m to a net asset of ₹1,906.75m**.
- **The total tax charge rose 6.5% to ₹5,777.74m while profit before tax rose 14.5%.** The effective tax rate (ETR) fell from 37.26% to 34.66%, but it is still about 9.5 percentage points above the 25.168% Indian statutory rate.
- **The biggest reason the ETR is high is losses on which no deferred tax asset is recognised.** This line added ₹1,426.83m in FY26, or 8.6 points of ETR. Carried-forward tax losses grew to about **₹29,400m**, and German losses alone rose 43.6% to ₹13,929.32m.
- **Several internal inconsistencies need management's explanation.** These include a ₹33.00m gap in the deferred tax P&L charge, the prior-year tax line showing different signs in two places, OCI components that do not add up for FY25, and mislabelled balance-sheet lines.
- **Current tax payable, advance tax and contingent tax demands are not in this extract.** The liability analysis below therefore covers only deferred tax and the P&L tax charge.

## Detailed analysis

### 1. P&L tax charge and effective tax rate

**The P&L tax charge ties out:**
- FY26: 5,701.41 − 95.34 + 171.67 = 5,777.74
- FY25: 5,994.80 − 146.26 − 423.04 = 5,425.50

**The reported ETR checks out:**
- FY26: 5,777.74 ÷ 16,671.72 = 34.66%
- FY25: 5,425.50 ÷ 14,558.25 = 37.27%. The report shows 37.26%, which is a minor rounding difference.

**Current tax as a share of profit before tax fell sharply:**
- FY26: 5,701.41 ÷ 16,671.72 = 34.20%
- FY25: 5,994.80 ÷ 14,558.25 = 41.18%

So the "cash-type" tax burden eased. The total charge still rose because deferred tax swung from a ₹423.04m credit to a ₹171.67m expense.

**Rate reconciliation (Note 21, p.405):**

| Exceptional items not deductible | 210.20 | 1.26% | – | – |
| Differential tax rates | (31.07) | (0.19%) | 68.37 | 0.47% |

The totals differ from the reported figures by ₹0.01m, which is rounding.

- **Main driver of the high ETR:** loss-making subsidiaries whose losses give no tax benefit. From the loss disclosures, these are mainly overseas (Germany and USA).
- **Why the ETR improved:** this drag shrank relative to a larger profit base, and other disallowances turned negative.
- **Exceptional items:** the ₹210.20m non-deductible amount implies roughly ₹835m of exceptional items, if the Indian rate applies (210.20 ÷ 25.168%). This is an estimate only; the note does not give the amount.

The roll-forward (p.407) also ties out: 1,198.28 − 143.07 − 1,338.69 + 56.87 − 26.43 + 468.28 = 215.24. The main movements are:
- **Hedge deferred tax of ₹(1,338.69)m.** This is the biggest single driver. The hedge position flipped from a ₹188.01m liability to a ₹1,117.70m deferred tax asset.
- **"Other adjustments" of +₹468.28m.** This is not explained.
- **A business combination adding ₹56.87m.** The extract gives no detail on the acquisition.

**Interpretation.** The fall in the deferred tax liability does not reflect better tax planning. It is mostly an accounting mirror of **unrealised hedge losses** in OCI. Those balances will reverse through P&L as the hedged transactions happen. The underlying liability from depreciation and asset timing differences is steady at around ₹1.5–2.1bn gross.

### 3. Deferred tax asset side
- The net asset grew by ₹220.56m (11.6%). This is driven by the new hedge asset of ₹1,117.70m.
- These increases were partly offset by:
  - lower unrealised-profit-on-inventory assets (1,311.76 → 1,087.20, down ₹224.56m, which matches the P&L line of 224.55);
  - timing differences under tax standards swinging to a liability of 760.56 (FY25: 114.21).
- The roll-forward ties out: −1,901.43 + 281.74 + 38.16 − 540.46 = −2,121.99.

- **Potential tax value is large.** At a rough 30% rate, ₹29.4bn of losses would be worth about ₹8–9bn of unrecognised tax assets. This is an illustration only; the note does not disclose the unrecognised deferred tax asset amount.
- **Upside:** if the German or US businesses turn profitable, these losses could be used and the ETR would fall.
- **Downside risk:** the Indian and US 20-year losses expire over time.

## Red flags & areas for follow-up

1. **The deferred tax P&L charge does not add up by ₹33.00m (p.406–407).**
   - The components sum to 120.74 + 224.55 − 206.62 = **138.67**, but the stated total is **171.67**.
   - The roll-forwards give the same 138.67 (DTA 281.74 − DTL 143.07).
   - The P&L and ETR reconciliation use 171.67.
   - FY25 ties exactly (−423.04).
   - The hedge balance swing (188.01 + 1,117.70 = 1,305.71) is also ₹32.98m below the OCI hedge credit of 1,338.69. This suggests a reclassification between P&L and OCI.
   - **Ask management to reconcile.**
2. **The prior-year tax line is inconsistent.** The P&L shows **(95.34)**, a credit, but the rate reconciliation shows **+130.06**, a charge. That is a ₹225.40m gap and a sign reversal. The FY25 figures agree (146.26 in both places).
3. **The FY25 OCI components do not tie.** −267.78 − 46.81 − 5.57 = **(320.16)** versus the stated total of **(309.02)**, a ₹11.14m difference. FY26 ties.
4. **Unexplained "Other adjustments".** These are +468.28 on the liability side and (540.46) on the asset side (net −72.18). Possible causes are currency translation, reclassification between entities, or offsetting. **Management should disclose what they are.**
5. **The balance-sheet presentation table is mislabelled (p.406).**
   - For FY26, "Deferred tax liabilities" within the asset group is shown as **(1,042.68)**, a negative liability.
   - Its FY25 counterpart (86.35) equals the PPE line.
   - Presentation needs to be clarified.
6. **The PPE deferred tax sign looks wrong.** The PPE balances fell by ₹120.74m in total (109.41 + 11.33), which implies a *credit*. The component table shows a +120.74 *expense*. This needs confirmation.
7. **US losses are described inconsistently.** Some are said to be available "indefinitely", yet the note also says the losses "will expire between tax years 2026 and 2037". Some US losses may be expiring right now.
8. **Typo:** Germany's FY25 figure is printed as "₹9.698.51 million" and should be 9,698.51.
9. **Sweden's losses fell by ₹2,362.83m (87%).** Were they used against profits, forfeited, or removed through a restructuring or disposal? If used, why was there no visible benefit in the rate reconciliation?
10. **Recoverability of the hedge deferred tax asset (₹1,117.70m).** It depends on the entity generating taxable profits when the hedges settle. This is also worth asking about.
11. **Information not in the extract:**
    - current tax liabilities or provisions, and advance tax / tax receivable balances;
    - contingent liabilities for disputed tax demands;
    - the amount of unrecognised deferred tax assets;
    - the exceptional items themselves.

    Obtain the balance sheet, the current tax note and the contingent liabilities note to complete the view of tax liabilities.
