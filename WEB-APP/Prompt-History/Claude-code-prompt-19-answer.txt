## Summary
- The CV business shrank in FY2026. Domestic CV grew 8% to ₹1,036 crore, but CV exports fell 34% to ₹1,324 crore (MD&A, pp.76, 78).
- My calculation from those two figures: CV revenue fell from about ₹2,965 crore to ₹2,360 crore, a drop of about 20%. The export fall (about ₹682 crore) was roughly seven times the domestic gain (about ₹77 crore).
- Management says the export fall came from cyclical de-stocking at North American Class 8 truck makers. North American CV revenue halved, with no loss of market share (Board's report, p.89).
- Management expects FY27 to be a year of growth. That is an outlook, not a result. It names diesel prices and geopolitics as risks (Board's report, p.89; MD&A, pp.76–77).
- The audited statements do not show CV revenue. Note 49 reports segments as Forgings, Defence and Others, not by vehicle type.

## What management says
- **Products and position:** CV exports focus on Class 7 and 8 trucks in the US and heavy commercial vehicles in Europe. The products are safety-critical engine, driveline and transmission parts. Management says getting quality right the first time, plus a focus on high-value components, makes Bharat Forge "a critical supplier to most of our OEM customers." It says these strengths give it long-lasting ties with all the major truck makers (MD&A, p.76).
- **Tariffs:** Management states that the US tariff regime, including Sec 232, caused "no business disruptions" and no loss of market share (MD&A, p.76; Board's report, p.89).
- **Domestic market:** According to management, India's GST rate cut lowered the total cost of owning a truck, and fleet operators spent the savings on new trucks. Better roads and manufacturing growth also helped. Trucks are getting bigger (tractor-trailers, tippers), which management says favours suppliers as truck makers build "bigger and sophisticated platforms" (MD&A, p.78).

## Performance and drivers
Prior-year figures below are back-calculated from the growth rates management gives:
- **Domestic CV:** ₹1,036 crore ÷ 1.08 ≈ ₹959 crore in FY25, so about +₹77 crore. Management attributes the gain to GST and infrastructure (MD&A, p.78).
- **Export CV:** ₹1,324 crore ÷ 0.66 ≈ ₹2,006 crore in FY25, so about −₹682 crore. Management attributes this to North American de-stocking (MD&A, p.76).
- **Total CV:** ₹2,360 crore against about ₹2,965 crore, a change of −20.4%. The export share of CV revenue fell from about 68% to 56%.
- **Size in context:** ₹2,360 crore is ₹23,600 million, about 14.0% of consolidated revenue of ₹168,116.53 million (P&L, p.342). It is about 16.9% of Forgings segment revenue of ₹139,285.82 million, down from about 22.9% (₹29,650m of ₹129,612.33m) (Note 49, p.473). Treat these as rough: the MD&A figures may cover a different group of entities than the consolidated accounts.

## Outlook and risks
- **Expectations:** Management says "the worst of the cyclical correction is behind us," so FY27 is "likely to be a year of growth" (Board's report, p.89). For India it cites industry forecasts (ICRA) of 3–6% auto growth and "stable demand in commercial vehicles," helped by ₹12.2 lakh crore of infrastructure spending (MD&A, p.77).
- **Named risks:**
  - A sharp rise in diesel prices could hurt domestic CV growth (MD&A, p.77).
  - West Asia tensions could raise shipping, energy and input costs (MD&A, p.76).
  - Trade uncertainty in North America and weak demand in Europe (Board's report, p.89).
- **Implied risks:**
  - The business depends heavily on the US Class 8 truck cycle.
  - The GST boost to domestic demand may be a one-off.
  - Management's "no share loss" claim cannot be checked from the material provided.

- The audited notes cannot confirm any CV figure. CV sits inside the Forgings segment, which grew 7.5% while CV fell about 20% by my estimate. Other businesses must therefore have more than made up for it.
- The group's USA revenue actually rose 3.4%. This does not strictly contradict a 50% fall in North American CV revenue, because Note 49 counts revenue by customer location and the USA line includes non-CV businesses. It does mean the CV decline cannot be seen in the audited figures.
- The MD&A figures are probably standalone (Bharat Forge Ltd only). The report does not say which basis it uses.

## Questions for management
1. On what basis are the CV figures prepared (standalone or consolidated), and what were the FY25 figures in ₹ crore?
2. How much North American CV revenue came from de-stocking versus lower truck production, and what signals support the FY27 recovery?
3. What evidence backs "no loss of market share," such as share of wallet or programme wins?
4. How much of the 8% domestic growth came from the GST cut, and how much is likely to last?
5. What were CV margins, and how did lower volumes affect Forgings profitability? Forgings profit rose only 5.4%, from ₹18,561.18m to ₹19,560.91m (Note 49, p.473).
