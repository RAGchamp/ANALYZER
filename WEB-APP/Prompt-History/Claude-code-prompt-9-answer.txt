## Summary

- **Net PPE grew strongly.** It rose 24.1% to ₹72,575.52m (Balance Sheet, p.341). About 47% of the ₹14,101.75m increase came from two non-organic sources: foreign-currency translation (net ₹3,928.65m) and the acquisition of a subsidiary (net ₹2,675.69m). Organic net growth was about ₹7,497m (+12.8%).
- **Capex has shifted from building to commissioning.** CWIP fell 27.5% to ₹12,513.26m as ₹12,646.53m was capitalised, against ₹4,397.41m in FY25. Accrued PPE capex fell to about ₹10,473m (FY25: about ₹18,643m). Capex/depreciation dropped from 2.34x to 1.19x.
- **Depreciation is growing in line with revenue.** PPE depreciation rose 10.3% to ₹8,770.47m, and total D&A of ₹9,709.24m ties exactly to the P&L (Note 30, p.413). D&A/revenue was flat at about 5.78%. There was no PPE impairment in FY26 (FY25: ₹419.42m on the e-bike CGU).
- **The note ties to the statements, with small rounding gaps.** FY26 net PPE and CWIP agree to the Balance Sheet exactly. The FY25 net PPE differs by ₹0.03m, and the capitalised borrowing cost differs by ₹0.03m between the table and the narrative.
- **Several items need follow-up.** These are:
  - depreciation charged on freehold land;
  - an unusual translation movement on electrical installations;
  - an unexplained ₹135.54m CWIP "other adjustment";
  - acquired assets brought in at gross cost with accumulated depreciation;
  - ₹530.79m of CWIP/IAUD older than 3 years (including ₹73.19m suspended);
  - stale LIBOR references in the borrowing-cost disclosure.

## Detailed analysis

### 1. Net PPE bridge (FY26)

| + Additions | 16,343.55 |

| **= Calculated closing** | **72,575.54** |
| Reported closing (Note 3; Balance Sheet) | 72,575.52 |

The ₹0.02m difference is rounding.

Arithmetic checks:
- **Gross cost roll-forward agrees:** 113,203.65 + 8,907.32 + 16,343.55 + 5,432.95 − 1,321.81 = 142,565.66.
- **Accumulated depreciation roll-forward is ₹0.02m off:** 54,729.91 + 4,978.67 + 2,757.26 + 8,770.47 − 1,246.19 = 69,990.12, against 69,990.14 reported. The sub-totals (A) 68,506.26 + (B) 1,483.87 = 69,990.13. This is rounding only.
- **The FY25 bridge also works:** 54,682.72 + 11,475.89 + 712.87 − 7,954.96 − 419.42 − 23.36 = 58,473.74.

### 2. Organic vs. inorganic growth

- **Reported net PPE growth** was ₹14,101.75m (+24.1%).
- **Less FX translation** of ₹3,928.65m. This is 27.9% of the growth and reflects rupee depreciation against the currencies of foreign subsidiaries. Translation on cost was 7.9% of opening gross cost (8,907.32 / 113,203.65), against 1.3% in FY25.
- **Less the acquisition** of ₹2,675.69m (19.0% of the growth).
- **Organic net growth** is therefore about ₹7,497.41m (+12.8%). That is broadly in line with revenue growth of 11.2% (168,116.53 vs 151,228.03, P&L p.342).

### 3. Capex cycle: CWIP converting into PPE

**Accrued PPE capex**
- FY26: 6,776.15 into CWIP + direct PPE additions of (16,343.55 − 12,646.53) = 3,697.02, giving **≈ ₹10,473.17m**.
- FY25: 11,564.63 + (11,475.89 − 4,397.41) = **≈ ₹18,643.11m**.

**Capex/depreciation**
- FY26: 10,473.17 / 8,770.47 = **1.19x**.
- FY25: 18,643.11 / 7,954.96 = **2.34x**.

The heavy FY24–25 build-out is now being commissioned. Of FY26 PPE additions, 82.3% is plant & machinery (13,454.82 / 16,343.55). Capex has slowed towards roughly maintenance-plus levels.

**CWIP roll-forward (FY26)**
- 17,265.72 + 598.98 + 6,776.15 + 275.40 − 12,646.53 − 135.54 + 391.33 = 12,525.51, against 12,525.49 reported. The ₹0.02m gap is rounding.
- Accumulated impairment of ₹12.23m on CWIP is unchanged.

**CWIP ageing (Note 3(h), p.379)**
- The 1–2 year bucket fell from 7,443.64 to 3,584.92.
- Balances older than 3 years rose from 390.13 to 530.79. This includes ₹73.19m of suspended projects, all now older than 3 years (FY25: 32.65 at 2–3 years plus 40.54 at more than 3 years).
- Management states that no project is overdue or over budget.

### 4. Depreciation intensity and asset age

**Implied depreciation rate on depreciable gross cost (excluding land)**
- FY26: 8,770.47 / avg(139,226.60; 111,082.53) = 8,770.47 / 125,154.57 = **7.0%**, an implied average life of about 14 years.
- FY25: 7,954.96 / avg(99,414.79; 111,082.53) = **7.6%**.
- The lower FY26 rate is partly mechanical. Year-end FX restatement and the mid-year acquisition inflate the closing gross cost, and assets capitalised during the year carry only part-year depreciation.

**Accumulated depreciation / gross cost**
- 45.6% (FY24), 48.3% (FY25) and 49.1% (FY26).
- For plant & machinery, the ratio is 54.9%, against 54.7% in FY25.
- The asset base is ageing slightly despite heavy capitalisation. This is partly because the acquired assets arrive about 51% depreciated (2,757.26 / 5,432.95).

**D&A / revenue**
- FY26: 9,709.24 / 168,116.53 = 5.78%.
- FY25: 8,736.19 / 151,228.03 = 5.78%. The ratio is flat.

### 5. Asset efficiency

**Revenue / average net PPE**
- FY26: 168,116.53 / 65,524.65 = **2.57x**.
- FY25: 151,228.03 / 56,578.25 = **2.67x**. The FY24 net block used here is 100,539.85 − 45,857.13 = 54,682.72.

**PPE + CWIP as a share of total assets**
- FY26: 85,088.78 / 222,599.55 = 38.2%.
- FY25: 75,727.26 / 200,883.21 = 37.7%.

The slight dip in turnover reflects newly commissioned capacity that has not yet reached full revenue run-rate, plus FX inflation of the asset base.

### 6. Disposals

- Gross disposals were ₹1,321.81m at cost, 94% depreciated (1,246.19). This points to retirement or sale of old plant & machinery.
- The resulting gain of ₹185.34m is plausible: proceeds 274.32 − NBV 75.62 = 198.70. The ₹13.36m gap is likely explained by intangibles or disposal costs.

### 7. Borrowing costs

- Borrowing costs capitalised were ₹391.33m, against P&L finance costs of ₹3,233.46m. Gross interest cost is therefore 3,624.79, and the capitalised share is 10.8% (FY25: 304.96 / 4,479.89 = 6.8%).
- The share rose even though CWIP fell. Its mid-year average of about ₹14,896m implies an approximate capitalisation yield of 2.6%, consistent with the low-spread foreign-currency loans cited in note (a).

## Red flags & areas for follow-up

1. **Depreciation on freehold land.** A charge of ₹2.96m plus FCTR of ₹3.42m gives accumulated depreciation of ₹6.38m on freehold land (p.377). Freehold land is normally not depreciated. In addition, the FCTR (3.42) exceeds the cumulative charge (2.96) on a nil opening balance, which is arithmetically odd. Ask management whether this is misclassified land improvements or a leasehold element.
2. **Electrical installations.** In FY26 this asset class shows FCTR of ₹140.88m on cost (32% of opening cost) and ₹96.17m on accumulated depreciation (46%), despite nil FCTR in FY25 and no acquisition additions. Its depreciation charge also jumped four-fold, from ₹18.30m to ₹73.99m. This suggests a mis-mapped reclassification rather than genuine translation.
3. **Unexplained CWIP "Other adjustments – Others" of ₹(135.54)m.** There is no matching PPE entry. Note (c) calls it a reclassification "within block of assets". Ask whether it went to intangibles under development, was expensed, or was written off.
4. **Acquired assets recorded gross with accumulated depreciation** (5,432.95 gross less 2,757.26 accumulated). Under Ind AS 103, acquired assets are recognised at fair value, which becomes their deemed cost. Confirm that the net ₹2,675.69m equals fair value per the purchase price allocation, and ask why goodwill (+₹1,274.70m) is so large relative to the assets acquired.
5. **Borrowing-cost disclosure issues:**
   - The table shows ₹391.33m but the narrative says ₹391.36m (₹0.03m difference).
   - The narrative says amounts are "transferred from CWIP", yet they appear as additions within the CWIP column.
   - The capitalisation rates still reference LIBOR, which ceased in 2023. The disclosure appears stale.
6. **FY25 net PPE.** The Balance Sheet shows ₹58,473.77m against ₹58,473.74m in Note 3. This is rounding but should be corrected.
7. **Ageing CWIP and suspended projects.**
   - ₹530.79m is older than 3 years.
   - ₹73.19m of suspended projects has been static for at least two years with no impairment beyond the ₹12.23m legacy provision.
   - Management states nothing is overdue. Ask about recoverability and the plans for the suspended projects.
8. **Assets lying with third parties tripled** to ₹989.51m (Note 3(b)). This likely covers dies and tooling at vendors. Ask about physical verification, insurance and the controls in place.
9. **Title deeds not in the Holding Company's name or unavailable.** The properties concerned have been held since 1977, 1981 and 1987. The amounts are immaterial (about ₹0.18m NBV), but these are long-standing unresolved issues.
10. **FY25 disposal loss does not reconcile.** NBV of disposals was ₹23.36m against proceeds of ₹65.40m, which implies a gain. The Cash Flow Statement instead shows a loss of ₹14.87m. Ask whether other asset classes or write-offs were netted in.
11. **Cash vs accrued capex gap.** FY25 accrued capex exceeded cash paid by about ₹4.1bn. Obtain the capital creditors and capital advances movements (Notes 19 and 14, not provided).
12. **Cash Flow Statement presentation (outside the PPE scope).** The cash reconciliation adds the entire FCTR reserve movement (₹2,205.34m) to reach closing cash (6,273.51 + 2,205.34 = 8,478.85). That movement mostly relates to PPE and other net assets, not cash. It looks like a plug, and the opening cash of FY25 (₹5,777.75m close vs 6,210.35) is similarly bridged.
13. **Information not provided:** Notes 5 (intangibles), 21 (deferred tax on PPE), 32 (exceptional items), 43 (right-of-use assets), and the acquisition note. Without these, the PPE-related deferred tax, the full exceptional-items breakdown and the purchase price allocation cannot be verified.
