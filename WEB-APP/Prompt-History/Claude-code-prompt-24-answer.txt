## Summary
- **What it is.** North America Agricultural Insurance is Chubb's smallest P&C segment, at about 5% of 2025 consolidated net premiums earned (NPE) (Business, p.7). The audited numbers agree: 2,919 / 53,014 = 5.5% (Note 19, p.206). It has two parts: Rain and Hail, which writes federally subsidized multiple-peril crop insurance (MPCI) and private crop-hail cover, and Chubb Agribusiness, which writes farm, ranch and commercial agriculture P&C (Business, p.7–8).
- **Profit improved sharply.** Underwriting income rose from $146m (2023) to $354m (2024) to $517m (2025). Segment income rose from $183m to $412m to $577m (Note 19, pp.206–208).
- **Premiums recovered in 2025 but are still below 2023.** Net premiums written (NPW) were $2,926m, up 8.3% on 2024, against $3,188m in 2023 (Note 19).
- **This is a catastrophe-like business.** Results depend on weather, crop yields and commodity prices set in February and October. Management states that it usually cannot estimate the year's result until the fourth quarter (MD&A, p.78–79).
- **The MD&A extract gives no results commentary for this segment.** The $492m and $575m figures on p.57 belong to the **Personal P&C** segment, not agriculture (see below).

## What management says
- **Products.**
  - MPCI covers revenue (price and yield), yield, margin, prevented planting and replant. It is run with the USDA's Risk Management Agency (RMA), under a Standard Reinsurance Agreement (SRA) with the Federal Crop Insurance Corporation (Business, p.7; MD&A, p.78).
  - Crop-hail is a private product written acre by acre in the U.S. and parts of Canada. Chubb sets its own rates for it using industry data (MD&A, p.79).
  - Agribusiness covers commercial agricultural processors and distributors, and farm and ranch operations from hobby farms to corporate farms, including equine (Business, p.8).
- **Market position.** Management states that Chubb has been "one of the leading writers of crop insurance in the U.S." since the 1980s and is diversified across U.S. regions (MD&A, p.78).
- **Competition.** In MPCI, all approved providers sell the same forms at the same rates. Chubb therefore competes on service and digital tools. Agribusiness competes with national and regional carriers (Business, p.8).
- **Risk controls.** Management lists:
  - the SRA's pro-rata and state stop-loss protections
  - third-party proportional and stop-loss reinsurance
  - crop derivatives
  - township liability limits on crop-hail (MD&A, p.78–79).

## Performance and drivers
All figures are in $m, from Note 19, pp.206–208.

| | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| NPW | 3,188 | 2,703 | 2,926 |
| NPE | 3,169 | 2,705 | 2,919 |
| Losses and loss expenses | 2,874 | 2,170 | 2,239 |
| Underwriting income | 146 | 354 | 517 |
| Segment income | 183 | 412 | 577 |

My calculations:
- **Premium growth.**
  - NPW: 2,926 / 2,703 − 1 = **+8.3%**.
  - NPE: 2,919 / 2,705 − 1 = **+7.9%**.
  - 2024 NPW fell 15.2% (2,703 / 3,188 − 1).
- **Loss ratio.** 2,239 / 2,919 = **76.7%**, against 80.2% in 2024 and 90.7% in 2023.
- **Combined ratio.** (2,239 + 169 − 6) / 2,919 = **82.3%**, against 86.9% in 2024 and 95.4% in 2023.
- **Segment income** grew 40% (577 / 412 − 1). Net investment income adds only $86m, so the gain comes from underwriting.

What drove it, per management: pricing moves with February commodity prices and their volatility, and losses depend on October harvest prices and yields (MD&A, p.79). That fits the 2024 premium drop and the better loss ratios. However, **the report sections provided do not explain either year's result for this segment**.

**Administrative expenses are negative** in every year: (6), (10) and (1). The reason is not disclosed in the material provided.

## Outlook and risks
- **Expectation.** Management states that the 2026 SRA (covering July 2025 to June 2026) has no significant changes, so it "does not impact Chubb's outlook on the crop program" (MD&A, p.78).
- **Risks named or implied:**
  - drought, moisture, hail and more severe storms linked to climate change
  - commodity prices falling between February and October
  - lower commodity prices or volatility shrinking premiums
  - regulatory dependence on the RMA and SRA terms
  - premiums and earnings concentrated in Q2–Q3, with the year's result known only in Q4 (MD&A, p.78–79).

## Questions for management
1. How much of 2025 underwriting income came from MPCI, crop-hail and Agribusiness respectively?
2. How much of the fall in the loss ratio from 90.7% to 76.7% came from better yields or prices, and how much from reinsurance or derivatives?
3. What caused the 15% drop in NPW in 2024, and how much of the 2025 rebound came from commodity prices rather than growth in insured acres?
