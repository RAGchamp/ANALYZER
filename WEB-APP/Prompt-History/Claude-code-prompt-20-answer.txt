## Summary
- Overseas General Insurance (OGI) is Chubb's international P&C business outside North America. It has two divisions: **Chubb International**, the retail arm, which includes Huatai P&C (about 87.2% owned), and **Chubb Global Markets (CGM)**, the London wholesale arm, which includes Lloyd's Syndicate 2488 (Item 1, p.8).
- OGI earned 27% of Chubb's 2025 net premiums (Item 1, p.8). The audited figures agree: $14,374m of $53,014m is 27.1% (Note 19, p.206).
- In 2025 net premiums earned grew 7.3%, underwriting income grew 18.7% to $2,156m and segment income grew 10.8% to $3,167m (Note 19, p.206–207).
- The MD&A excerpt provided does **not** discuss OGI's 2025 results. The $223m, $179m and $214m figures on p.58 belong to the **Agricultural** segment, not OGI (see below).

## What management says
- **Chubb International** sells commercial P&C to large, middle-market and small customers, plus consumer A&H and personal lines, in local markets outside the U.S., Bermuda and Canada (Item 1, p.8; MD&A, p.59).
- **CGM** is a London wholesale business writing international specialty and excess-and-surplus lines. Syndicate 2488 is wholly owned and has £630m of underwriting capacity for the 2026 Lloyd's account year (Item 1, p.8).
- **Combined International** (supplemental A&H) no longer writes new business (Item 1, p.8).
- **Expansion:** in 2025 Chubb bought LMG Insurance in Thailand (Item 1, p.8). The audited note describes it as Liberty Mutual's Thai P&C business, included from April 1, 2025 (Note 19, p.204).
- **Coverage mix:** financial lines, marine, energy, aviation, political risk, construction, group A&H and personal lines. Business comes through retail brokers and agents, and through wholesale brokers in London (Note 19, p.204).
- The excerpts do not say anything specific to OGI about its competitive position.

## Performance and drivers
All figures are in $ millions, from Note 19 (p.206–208 and p.209). The ratios are my own calculations.

- **Premiums:** net premiums written were 15,024 against 13,972 (+1,052, **+7.5%**). Net premiums earned were 14,374 against 13,400 (+974, **+7.3%**) and 12,231 in 2023 (+17.5% over two years).
- **By line of business (NPE):**
  - Property & short-tail: 4,689 against 4,338 (+8.1%)
  - Casualty: 3,809 against 3,705 (+2.8%)
  - Personal lines: 3,198 against 2,785 (**+14.8%**)
  - A&H: 2,678 against 2,572 (+4.1%)
- **Underwriting:**
  - Combined ratio: (6,589 + 470 + 3,724 + 1,435) / 14,374 = **85.0%**, against 11,583 / 13,400 = 86.4% in 2024.
  - Loss ratio (losses plus policy benefits): 49.1% against 50.9%.
  - Expense ratio: 35.9% against 35.5%.
  - The improvement came from losses, while expenses rose slightly.
- **Segment income:** 3,167, which is 22.3% of total segment income of 14,227. Net investment income was flat (1,139 against 1,136). Other expense rose from 14 to 50.
- **Geography:** Asia accounts for 20% of group NPE and Europe for 11% (Note 19, p.209). These are group-wide figures, not OGI alone.
- **What drove these changes is not explained in the material provided.** The MD&A analysis of OGI's results was not in the excerpts. The acquisition in Thailand added part of the growth, but its size is not disclosed.

## Outlook and risks
- The excerpts contain no outlook for OGI.
- The text points to these risks:
  - **Foreign currency:** the currency translation adjustment in OCI was +1,047 in 2025 against −1,177 in 2024 (P&L, p.108).
  - **Catastrophe exposure:** property and short-tail lines are the largest line (33% of OGI's NPE).
  - **Integration** of the Thai acquisition.
  - **China:** concentration through Huatai, with its minority interest.
  - **Lloyd's:** capital must be provided to support Syndicate 2488's capacity.

## Questions for management
