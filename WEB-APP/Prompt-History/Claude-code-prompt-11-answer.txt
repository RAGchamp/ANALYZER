# Capital Creditors, Capital Advances and the Capex Gap: Follow-up

## Summary

- **Only some of the requested items are in the material.** Notes 14, 19 and 5 give FY26 and FY25 balances. FY24 balances, the split of the CWIP "Disposals/Capitalised" line, a non-cash investing disclosure and the 31-Mar-2024 Balance Sheet are **not provided**.
- **This corrects my previous answer.** Capital creditors are only ₹855.03m at 31-Mar-25 and ₹1,158.51m at 31-Mar-26, all current (Note 19, p.402). I previously suggested that about ₹3.2bn of FY25–26 capex was still unpaid to suppliers. That is **not supported**: at most ₹1,158.51m is owed to suppliers of capital goods.
- **The FY25 gap is still largely unexplained.** Supplier credit can explain at most about ₹0.9bn of it. The rest (at least ₹3.6bn) would have to come from falling capital advances (cash paid in FY24 or earlier), non-cash additions, or other factors. None of these can be verified without FY24 data.
- **FY26 does not reconcile either.** After adding intangibles and adjusting for creditors and advances, cash capex is about **₹1,056m higher** than the accrual-based figure.
- **The Note 19 jump is explained.** The ₹3,161.86m rise in non-current other financial liabilities is the ₹3,294.72m **gross liability on a written put option** over subsidiary CCPS. This confirms my earlier inference that it is not capital creditors.

To close the remaining gap through capital advances alone, **FY24 capital advances would need to have been at least ₹5,728.93m** (2,126.30 + 3,602.63). That would mean a fall of about 63% in FY25. This is plausible at the tail end of a heavy capex cycle: assets delivered in FY25 against cash prepaid in FY24 or earlier. It **needs the FY24 Note 14 to confirm.**

**Implication, which replaces my earlier view.** The FY25 gap most likely reflects **cash spent in earlier years** (via advances) or non-cash additions. It is **not** material deferred payment to suppliers. My earlier comment that FY25 free cash flow was "flattered" by about ₹4.1bn of unpaid capex should be read as a **timing shift from prior years**, not a future cash outflow. The only future outflow evidenced is the ₹1,158.51m of capital creditors.

## 8. Note 19 also resolves the non-current liability jump

- **The liability:** gross liability on a written put option of ₹3,294.72m = CCPS proceeds of ₹3,000.00m (Cash Flow, p.347) + fair-value change of ₹294.72m (SOCE, p.344).
- **Issue price check:** 14,25,795 CCPS × ₹2,104.09 = ₹3,000.0m (Note 19#, p.402), which agrees.
- **Accounting:** the Group uses the anticipated acquisition method. The NCI is derecognised and fair-value changes go to **other equity, not the P&L** (Note 19#, p.403).
- **SOCE check:** retained earnings roll 61,811.48 + 10,796.61 + 170.69 − 294.72 − 2,868.53 − 956.18 = 68,659.35. This agrees to the closing balance (SOCE, p.344), confirming the ₹294.72m was charged to retained earnings.

## 9. Other issues found in the newly provided notes

1. **FY25 IAUD outflow (399.26) exceeds intangible additions (321.81).** At least ₹77.45m left IAUD without being capitalised. Where was it charged?
2. **Refined disposal-gain check, including intangibles:**
   - FY26: proceeds 274.32 − PPE NBV 75.62 − intangibles NBV 1.88 (34.48 − 32.60) = 196.82, against a reported gain of 185.34. Difference: 11.48.
   - FY25: NBV 23.36 + 18.30 (78.80 − 60.50) = 41.66 against proceeds of 65.40, implying a gain of 23.74. The Cash Flow Statement shows a **loss of 14.87**, a difference of 38.61 (Cash Flow, p.346). This is still unreconciled.
3. **Dividends don't tie to cash.**
   - Declared: 2,868.53 + 956.18 = 3,824.71 (SOCE, p.344).
   - Paid: 3,796.55 (Cash Flow, p.347).
   - That implies unpaid dividend should rise by 28.16, but it **fell** by 68.76 (Note 19, p.402).
   - Unexplained difference: about ₹96.92m. Possibly dividends on shares held by the group, or TDS timing.
4. **"Derivative liability" of ₹32.50m is classified at amortised cost** (Note 19, p.402). Derivatives are normally carried at fair value. It is also unchanged for two years.
5. **Goodwill inconsistencies (Note 5, p.381–383):**
   - Acquisition goodwill is 1,224.24 in the table but 1,224.23 in note (d), a rounding difference.
   - MGL goodwill rose by 137.27 (192.31 → 329.58), but total net FX on goodwill was only 50.46 (98.80 − 48.34).
   - JS Autocast's terminal growth rate jumps from 1.00% to 5.00%, yet it is described as "in line with long-term inflation".

## Impact on the financial statements

- **Profit and Loss:**
  - Capital creditors and advances have no P&L effect, and **no extended credit terms** (and so no embedded financing cost) are disclosed.
  - The put-option fair-value change (₹294.72m) bypasses the P&L by policy choice.
  - Possible CWIP or IAUD write-offs (≥ ₹77.45m IAUD in FY25) would sit in exceptional items or other expenses. The location is unconfirmed.
- **Balance Sheet:**
  - Capital creditors of ₹1,158.51m (current other financial liabilities) and capital advances of ₹1,813.03m (other non-current assets) are both reconciled to their Balance Sheet totals (p.341).
  - The non-current other financial liabilities jump is the put liability, not capex funding.
- **Cash Flow:**
  - FY26 cash capex of ₹11,404.96m exceeds the expected ₹10,348.54m by ₹1,056.42m, which is unexplained.
  - The FY25 cash-versus-accrual gap (≥ ₹4.46bn) is **not** future supplier debt. Supplier credit explains at most ₹0.86bn. The rest most likely reflects earlier-year advances, which would mean FY24 and earlier investing cash flows carried that spend.
  - Future cash outflow evidenced for capex: ₹1,158.51m.
- **Changes in Equity:**
  - Capex timing has no effect.
  - The CCPS/put arrangement reduced retained earnings by ₹294.72m (SOCE, p.344). The ₹3,000m proceeds did not increase NCI, which stays at ₹(230.16)m.
